Possibility C: let prices rise. Inflation makes old debt smaller, because the dollars paid back buy less than the dollars borrowed. That also helped after World War Two. In nineteen forty-six alone, prices jumped about eighteen percent, quietly shrinking what the debt was worth. The catch: it isn't free. Rising prices hit everyone's grocery bill. And lenders aren't fools. If they expect inflation, they ask for higher rates on new loans. So envelope five can come right back, fatter than before.
