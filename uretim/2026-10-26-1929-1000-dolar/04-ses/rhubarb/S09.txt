On December eleventh, nineteen thirty, the fourth-largest bank in New York City, the Bank of United States, stops operating. Picture the street outside. A long line of people, all asking for their money at the same time. Here's the problem. A bank doesn't keep your thousand dollars in a box with your name on it. It lends most of it out. So when everyone comes at once, even a healthy bank can run out of cash. And when one bank falls, people at the next bank get nervous, and the line moves there.
