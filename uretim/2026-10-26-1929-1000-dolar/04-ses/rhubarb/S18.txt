Winter, nineteen thirty-three. Panic jumps from state to state. Visitors who come to Washington for the new president's first day find notes in their hotel rooms: checks from out-of-town banks won't be accepted. By March fourth, every state has declared a bank holiday. Two days later, the new president, Franklin Roosevelt, closes every bank in the nation. Good banks and bad banks, all shut. Bank Leo stands at a locked door. For a few days, cash at home is suddenly very useful, and Box Leo is the only player holding any. When the holiday ends, about four thousand banks never reopen. Then, in June nineteen thirty-three, a new law creates federal deposit insurance. It starts on January first, nineteen thirty-four, and covers up to two thousand five hundred dollars per depositor.
