Here's the part that surprises people today. In nineteen twenty-nine, a bank account in America had no federal deposit insurance. If your bank closed, nobody promised you'd get your money back. And there were a lot of banks. When the trouble began, over eight thousand banks belonged to the Federal Reserve System, and nearly sixteen thousand did not. Many were small and local. For a year, Bank Leo is fine. Then, in late nineteen thirty, banks start to fail in waves. Within a few weeks, hundreds close their doors.
